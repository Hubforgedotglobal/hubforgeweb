ACTIVE · FREE IN BOTH DIRECTIONS

Eval Corps

The glocal volunteer movement of evaluators and first-time explorers.

Pro bono MERL support for nonprofits, grassroots groups and social enterprises that cannot afford evaluation help. Volunteers give their skills under a written scope and signed standards. Newcomers get a mentor and a supervised first project.

WHY LEARNING GETS STUCK

Good questions need room to travel.

Research on locally led development points to conditions that interrupt the move from community knowledge to shared decisions. The systems map below traces how they connect.

01

Questions set elsewhere

Communities may be asked to report against priorities they did not define.

02

Reporting over learning

Compliance can crowd out joint interpretation and useful adjustment.

03

Skills without continuity

Training often lacks the time, mentorship and repeated practice that make skills stick.

04

Routes narrowed by systems

Procurement and professional networks can be harder for local and early-career evaluators to access.

Based on OECD guidance on locally led monitoring, evaluation and learning.

R1 · REINFORCING LOOP · HYPOTHESIS

2027 TO 2036 · ADAPTIVE PATHWAY

A proposal with exits, not a forecast.

Each phase starts only if its condition is met. Nothing here describes a confirmed partnership or result.

  1. 2027 to 2029ValidateStarting point

    Test the loops with a plural reference group of communities, public bodies, funders, VOPEs and universities. Map competing accounts separately before synthesis.

    Proceed only if partners can name links they recognise, links they reject, and observations that weaken them.
  2. 2030 to 2032ReplicateIf evidence supports it

    Carry what held up to new places and languages with local hosts. Drop links the evidence weakened.

    Proceed only if at least some links show the expected direction in more than one context, with rival explanations considered.
  3. 2033 to 2036InstitutionaliseIf gains are sustained

    Hand stewardship of shared tools, methods and maps to local and public institutions able to hold them.

    Success looks like HubForge being less central, not more.

SUPPORT SHARED MERL INFRASTRUCTURE

Fund the plumbing, not just the project.

Most funding follows a single programme. HubForge builds what many programmes share: open platforms, practical learning, volunteer evaluation support and routes into the field. Support here keeps that infrastructure open to the organisations least able to pay for it.

01
Who benefits

Nonprofits, grassroots groups, emerging evaluators and the communities they work with.

02
What support enables

Open platforms kept free, new Playbooks, volunteer matching and mentored practice.

03
How we stay accountable

Written scopes, a public Impact Log for Eval Corps and plain reporting on what changed.

FUNDER BRIEF

Grassroots and local organisations often lack time, money and practical support to build evidence. Learning and mentoring in MERL and technology can be expensive or hard to find, so community knowledge struggles to reach funding, advocacy and policy decisions.

Nonprofits and grassroots groups, emerging evaluators and MEL learners, VOPEs and educators, and through their work, the communities whose questions evidence should answer.

Four open platforms (EvalCanvas, Sampling Shala, EvalQR, EvalStudio), the Playbooks library, Eval Corps pro bono support with mentored pathways, at-cost advisory for nonprofits and preparation for the Glocal Impact Awards 2027.

Hosting, maintenance and accessibility of the platforms; new Playbooks and translations; volunteer matching and mentoring in Eval Corps; co-created pilots and convenings. Priorities are agreed with each funder rather than fixed in advance.

Together we would agree a written scope, the activities and outputs to track, and a reporting rhythm in plain language. What counts as progress is decided at the start, with honest reporting on what did and did not change.

A description of current work and intent, not a claim of measured impact. Eligibility and terms are confirmed in conversation.
