What Eval Corps is

Eval Corps is HubForge's free MERL support programme, rebuilt and renamed. It is a glocal volunteer movement, global in reach and local in roots, of evaluators, MERL professionals, technologists, and first-time explorers. Volunteers give their skills pro bono to nonprofits, grassroots groups, and social enterprises that cannot afford evaluation support.

The problem it exists to solve

Somewhere right now, extraordinary work is going unmeasured, unheard, and unlearned from. That is one gap seen from three sides, not three separate problems:

Organizations

Nonprofits, grassroots groups, and social enterprises around the world lack access to quality Monitoring, Evaluation, Research & Learning expertise. Their work is harder to prove, learn from, and grow.

Professionals

Thousands of experienced evaluators, researchers, data professionals, and technologists want meaningful ways to contribute their knowledge beyond their day jobs. There is no simple way to do it.

Young people

Many who want to explore MERL have nowhere to start: no mentor, no real project, no structured way to find out whether evaluation is the work they want to build a career on.

Eval Corps exists to close that gap by bringing all three worlds together.

Vision

We believe the world doesn't just need better evidence. It needs a movement of people who refuse to let good work go unmeasured, unheard, and unlearned from, and a real door in for the next generation who want to learn how.

This is bigger than any single evaluation. We're building the people, the practice, and the movement that outlasts us all.

The three promises

01

Management: you will always know what you signed up for

Every role has a written scope before you start: what you'll do, how long it takes, and what "done" looks like. Every volunteer has a named point of contact, not a general inbox. Onboarding takes no more than one session. Two to three hours a week means two to three hours a week.

02

Experience: you belong here, wherever you're starting from

A first-year MERL student and a twenty-year evaluator both have a real seat, by design. Recognition is built into the workflow, with mentorship in both directions.

03

Impact: you will see what your time built

Every engagement closes with a visible outcome, and a running, plain-language Impact Log shows the cumulative footprint, including your piece of it.

The line we hold. We would rather grow slower and keep this promise than grow fast and break it. If Eval Corps ever has to choose between recruiting more volunteers and taking care of the ones we have, we choose the second.

How volunteers join

Four pathways, chosen by intent rather than job title:

Learn the field

I'm new to MERL and want a structured, mentored start.

Give my skills

I'm a practicing professional; put my craft to work.

Build the community

I write, host, organize, and connect people.

Lead the movement

I want founding-team responsibility for a workstream.

The specific role is assigned by leadership afterwards, in a conversation, never silently.

The role catalog

Fifteen roles across three tiers, each with a written time commitment and a definition of done:

Explorer tier (1 role)

Eval Corps Explorer.

Contributor tier (9 roles)

Mentor, Storyteller, Convener, Consultant, Toolkit Contributor, Reviewer, Translator, Event Support, Technologist.

Leadership tier (5 roles)

Strategy, MERL Standards, Community, Partnerships, Knowledge.

What happens after applying

A real person replies within seven days, and that is a metric Eval Corps tracks, not a pleasantry. Nobody is declined for inexperience alone; inexperience routes to the Explorer track, which comes with a mentor and a supervised first project. Accepted volunteers get a written role scope, a named contact, one onboarding session, a mid-engagement check-in, and a closing conversation with an Impact Log entry. A contribution letter is available on request.

For organizations

Free pro bono MERL support: evaluation design, data review, or framework input from experienced volunteer professionals. Delivered under a written scope and signed confidentiality standards. Costs nothing.

Ecosystem position

Eval Corps is a signatory to the Youth in Evaluation manifesto, the Eval4Action campaign's commitment to long term, meaningful engagement of young and emerging evaluators. Its work is aligned to the Global Evaluation Agenda 2.0.
